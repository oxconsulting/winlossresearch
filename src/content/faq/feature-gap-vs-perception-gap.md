# What's the difference between a feature gap and a perception gap?

A feature gap is a real product capability a vendor does not have, and a competitor's version of it genuinely won the deal. A perception gap is a capability the vendor already has, but the buyer never learned that during the evaluation, so the deal was lost to a false impression rather than a real limitation. CRM data logs both the same way, as a lost feature comparison, which is why the two get confused.

Reps log "feature gap" after a competitive loss because it's a clean, defensible reason. It points accountability at the product roadmap rather than at how the deal was run, and it requires no further explanation on the rep's part. That logging habit produces a dataset that systematically undercounts perception problems, not because those losses are rare, but because they never resolve cleanly into a CRM dropdown.

Integrations are where this shows up most reliably. Almost no buyer tests an integration before signing. They go entirely on what the vendor shows or describes during the evaluation. A deal logged as "integration gap" is rarely about an absolute absence of the capability. More often, one vendor told a more confident story about the same integration and the buyer's perception filled in the rest.

The fixes are not interchangeable. A genuine feature gap belongs on the product roadmap, and no amount of better messaging closes it. A perception gap belongs with sales enablement and product marketing, because the capability already exists and the evaluation simply failed to surface it. Sending a real feature gap to the messaging team wastes a cycle. Sending a perception gap to the product team builds something that already shipped.

Perception gaps rarely surface through rep-sourced data, because buyers don't frame their objections that way. A buyer who says "we weren't confident it could handle our scale" sounds to the rep like a product limitation, and it gets logged as one. Independent win/loss interviews ask directly what the buyer understood by the end of the evaluation, which is the only reliable way to find out whether a capability was missing or simply invisible.

Some losses are genuine feature gaps and belong in the next roadmap conversation. The blind spot worth naming is the losses that aren't, where the vendor had the capability and the buyer either never saw it or never believed it. Before the next feature request lands on a product team's desk, it is worth confirming that buyers actually knew the capability was there.
