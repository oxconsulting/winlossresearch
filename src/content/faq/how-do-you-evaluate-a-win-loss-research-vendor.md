# How do you evaluate a win/loss research vendor?

Evaluate a win/loss research vendor primarily on its deliverable model: whether it produces synthesized findings across a full interview set, or simply hands back individual deal summaries with no connective analysis. Interview volume and reporting cadence are easy to compare but say little about whether the vendor turns interviews into decisions your team can act on.

This single distinction separates real win/loss research from what amounts to deal-summaries-as-a-service, and it's worth checking before comparing anything else about a vendor's process.

## The Deliverable Model Test

Some vendors deliver a write-up per interview: well-written, thorough, and disconnected from every other interview in the set. The client's team receives a folder of individual accounts and is left to notice a pattern across them, if anyone has the bandwidth to read all of them and connect the dots. This looks like effort and reads like quality work, but it's compilation, not research.

A vendor doing real win/loss research delivers fewer, less frequent reports, and each one pulls a specific, actionable conclusion across a dozen or more interviews: a competitor consistently winning late-stage evaluations on a particular capability rather than price, three cycles running, or a message that tests well in demos but consistently fails to survive an internal buying conversation. That's a finding a team can act on the same week it lands. A stack of individual recaps, however polished, isn't.

Ask a prospective vendor directly what their deliverable contains before asking about their interview volume or their cadence. The answer to that question tells you more about the quality of the engagement than either of the other two metrics.

## Interview Quality and Neutrality

Beyond the deliverable, evaluate whether the vendor's interviews are adaptive or scripted. A vendor running a fixed survey instrument produces the same limited candor problem an internal team faces: buyers optimize answers for politeness rather than accuracy when the format doesn't allow for follow-up probing. A skilled independent interviewer adjusts questions in real time based on what a buyer says, following up on a vague answer rather than moving to the next scripted item.

Confirm the vendor's neutrality is structural, not just stated. The value of third-party research comes specifically from having no relationship to protect and no sale at stake in the deals being reviewed. A vendor with any commercial relationship to the deals under review, directly or through a parent company, reintroduces the same conflict internal programs are meant to avoid.

## Methodology and Program Fit

A credible vendor can explain its interview count and ratio (commonly 20 to 30 interviews, weighted toward losses), its research timing window relative to deal close, and how it handles deal selection without letting the client's sales team curate which deals get reviewed. Vendors who can't articulate why they land on a specific number, ratio, or timing window are working from convention rather than a defensible methodology.
