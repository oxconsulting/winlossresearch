# How do you identify patterns in win/loss research?

A pattern in win/loss research is identified when the same concern, objection, or competitive comparison recurs independently across multiple buyer interviews, described in different language by buyers who never spoke to each other. A single mention is a deal anecdote; the same finding appearing across a meaningful share of the interview set is a pattern.

## Why Independence Is the Key Requirement

The critical word is independently. If two buyers happen to describe a similar concern because they compared notes during the sales process, or because both were responding to the same recent product change, that's not the same as two buyers, evaluating separately and unaware of each other's answers, independently landing on the same concern. Independent recurrence is what gives a pattern its evidentiary weight. A researcher tracking themes across an interview set has to keep this distinction in mind, since coincidental agreement can look identical to a genuine pattern on the surface.

## How Patterns Typically Emerge Across an Interview Set

Early interviews in a program surface tentative themes: a pricing concern here, a demo objection there. Something mentioned casually in an early [loss interview](/glossary/loss-interview/) often reappears, unprompted and with more specificity, several interviews later. By the later stretch of a full [interview count](/glossary/interview-count/), a theme that has reappeared independently across a meaningful portion of the set has moved from tentative observation to something closer to confirmed finding.

## Testing a Candidate Pattern Against the Win Set

Identifying a pattern in loss interviews is only half the process. Checking whether the same concern shows up in [win interviews](/glossary/win-interview/), and if so, how, sharpens what the pattern actually means. A concern that appears only in losses points to a specific, unaddressed gap. The same concern appearing in wins as something buyers noticed but tolerated suggests a real but lower-urgency issue. Skipping this comparison risks treating every pattern as equally urgent, when the win-set comparison often reveals they aren't.

## What Keeps a Pattern From Being Mistaken for Noise

Volume matters here specifically because a small interview set makes it hard to tell a genuine, recurring pattern from a coincidental cluster of similar-sounding but unrelated comments. A standard [interview count](/glossary/interview-count/) of 20 to 30, weighted toward losses, exists in large part to give a pattern enough independent supporting evidence to be trusted rather than dismissed as noise.

## A Practical Example of the Process

A researcher notices a passing comment in interview five about a buyer needing to build a manual workaround for a missing integration. On its own, that's a [deal anecdote](/glossary/deal-anecdote/), not a finding. By interview fourteen, two more buyers have independently described the same underlying gap, each using different language and each unaware of what the others said. That independent recurrence, three buyers, unprompted, describing the same underlying issue, is what moves the observation from an interesting detail to a pattern worth including in the final report and checking against the win interview set for confirmation.
