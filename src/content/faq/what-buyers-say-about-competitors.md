# What do buyers say about competitors your team won't hear?

Buyers describe specific, decision-stage competitor advantages to a neutral researcher that they never say directly to a vendor's sales team, such as a competitor's rep being more responsive, a demo feeling more tailored to their actual problem, or simply connecting better with the other rep as a person. Reps mostly hear the generic, discovery-stage version, since buyers soften anything that would feel too personal to say to the team they just declined.

[Competitive perception](/glossary/competitive-perception/) shifts substantially between the discovery stage of an evaluation and the final decision. Early in a deal, a buyer's read on the competitive landscape is broad and easy to share in an ordinary sales conversation: general reputation, apparent feature parity, a rough sense of pricing. By the time a decision gets made, that read has sharpened into something far more specific, formed after multiple calls and real comparative experience, and it's exactly the version buyers are least likely to volunteer to a rep they're actively declining.

A rep rarely hears that a competitor's sales process felt more organized, that a rival demo felt tailored to the buyer's actual problem while the vendor's felt generic, or that a competitor simply followed up faster during a critical week of the evaluation. These reasons feel personal to share directly with the team that just lost the business, so buyers default to something more neutral: pricing, a specific feature, a timeline mismatch. The version that actually explains the loss stays private precisely because the rep was in the room.

A neutral third party gets a different account, because there's no relationship left to protect once the deal has closed and the researcher has no stake in either outcome. A buyer will describe exactly what a competitor did that a vendor's team didn't, in a level of detail that never reaches a CRM through a rep debrief, because none of it was ever said to the rep to begin with.

That gap compounds over time. Every loss where the real competitive story stays private becomes another data point supporting whatever narrative a team already has about a given competitor, and the next battlecard update inherits a version of events assembled from softened feedback rather than buyer truth.

The discovery-versus-decision distinction is worth tracking deliberately, because most CI programs only ever capture the earlier version. A buyer might describe a competitor generically and favorably during an early call, then later reveal that the real reason a deal tipped had nothing to do with product or price at all, but with something as specific as a competitor's team following up faster during one critical week of the evaluation. That kind of detail almost never survives a rep debrief, because a buyer has no reason to volunteer it to the vendor's own sales team, and a rep has no way to ask for it without the conversation feeling adversarial. Independent interviews remove that awkwardness by design, since the researcher has no stake in either outcome and the buyer has no relationship left to protect.
