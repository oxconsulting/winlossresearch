# What's the difference between what buyers say and think?

Buyers soften what they say to a vendor to preserve the relationship, avoid friction, or end an uncomfortable conversation cleanly. What they actually think, the full unsoftened account of what drove a decision, only surfaces once no one connected to the vendor is in the room.

## The Gap Is Not Dishonesty

Buyers answering a vendor's questions aren't lying. They're managing a [managed conversation](/glossary/managed-conversation/): giving true but incomplete answers, calibrated to what feels safe to say to someone with a stake in the response. A buyer might genuinely believe pricing was the deciding factor when asked by a sales rep, while a fuller account, drawn out by someone with no connection to the vendor, reveals a stalled technical evaluation or an internal stakeholder the rep never met. Both answers can be sincere. Only one is complete.

Surveys run into a related but distinct problem: self-selection. One buyer, reflecting on a survey her vendor had run through an outside platform, put it this way: "I think it is a great idea that the vendor brought in a third party for this. If I'm being honest, I've told you some things I probably didn't bring up with them and probably wouldn't have." The vendor had already taken the step of using an outside platform, and the buyer still distinguished between what she'd tell that process and what she'd tell an independent researcher with no reporting line back to the vendor at all.

## Why the Gap Closes With Independence

The mechanism is the same one that separates [third-party win/loss research](/glossary/third-party-win-loss-research/) from an [internal win/loss program](/glossary/internal-win-loss-program/). A buyer's calculation about what to disclose depends entirely on who's listening and what happens to the answer afterward. Remove any stake the listener has in the sale, the relationship, or the outcome, and the buyer no longer needs to manage the response. What was previously filtered for safety gets reported for accuracy instead.

This has a direct consequence for how findings should be sourced. A finding built entirely from what buyers say to a vendor's own team, however thoroughly documented, reflects the managed version by construction. What buyers actually think, the version that explains the pattern behind a set of wins and losses rather than the polite summary of it, requires removing the vendor from the conversation.
