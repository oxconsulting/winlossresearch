# What questions does win/loss research answer?

Win/loss research answers why specific deals were won or lost, and, more importantly, what patterns repeat across a full population of deals rather than any single one. It covers buying process, competitive positioning, pricing perception, messaging resonance, and the specific objections that shaped each outcome.

## The Core Question It's Built to Answer

At its center, win/loss research answers a single question a GTM leader can't reliably answer from internal data alone: what actually determined the outcome of this deal, from the buyer's own account, rather than the vendor's interpretation of it. Every other question a program addresses branches off that core one.

## The Specific Questions a Program Typically Covers

A [win/loss interview](/glossary/win-loss-interview/), guided by a structured [interview questionnaire](/glossary/interview-questionnaire/), typically surfaces answers to: how the buying process actually unfolded and who was involved in the internal decision, what evaluation criteria the buyer used and how the vendor measured up, how the vendor compared to competitors the buyer considered, whether pricing was perceived as fair or confusing, whether the vendor's messaging and positioning actually landed, and what specific objection or concern was decisive in the outcome.

## What It Doesn't Answer on Its Own

A single interview answers these questions for one deal. The questions win/loss research is actually valuable for, at the program level, are broader: what competitive concern is costing the most deals across the pipeline, whether a messaging change is landing with buyers, whether a specific product gap is a genuine dealbreaker or a minor friction point. Those answers require [pattern recognition](/glossary/pattern-recognition/) across a full interview set, not any single conversation, however detailed.

## How These Questions Map to Different Stakeholders

Different questions matter more to different audiences within a GTM organization. A Competitive Intelligence leader is usually most interested in how the vendor stacked up against specific competitors and what buyers actually noticed during the comparison. A Product Marketing leader cares more about whether specific messaging and positioning landed as intended. A CMO or VP of Sales is often most focused on the pricing and process questions, since those tend to have the clearest connection to close rate. A single win/loss program can answer all of these simultaneously, but the emphasis in how findings get presented often shifts depending on which stakeholder is in the room.

## How the Answer Shapes Program Design

Because the questions a program is meant to answer vary by client, a well-designed [interview questionnaire](/glossary/interview-questionnaire/) is built around the specific GTM questions that matter to that engagement, rather than a fixed, generic list. A program scoped to understand competitive displacement asks different follow-up questions than one scoped to test whether a recent messaging change is resonating, even though both use the same underlying interview structure.
