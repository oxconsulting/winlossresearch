# Why do buyers open up more to a third-party researcher?

Buyers open up more to a third-party researcher because there is no relationship to protect and no sale at stake. When a vendor conducts the debrief, buyers soften feedback, credit a safe reason like price, and leave out the detail that actually drove the decision. An independent researcher removes that calculus entirely.

## The Calculation Buyers Are Actually Making

A buyer who just turned down a vendor, or who just became a paying customer, is still managing that relationship after the decision is made. They may want to preserve the option of revisiting the vendor in a future cycle, avoid an uncomfortable conversation with someone they otherwise liked, or protect a colleague or team from criticism that could reflect on them internally. None of this requires the buyer to be dishonest. It's simply what people do when the person asking the question has something at stake in the answer.

Remove the vendor from the interview and that calculation disappears. A buyer speaking with an [independent win/loss researcher](/glossary/independent-win-loss-researcher/) has no reason to soften a detail that might be uncomfortable for the vendor to hear, because there's no relationship on the line and no future interaction to protect.

## What This Looks Like in an Interview

Buyers frequently signal the shift directly. Some ask outright whether the conversation is truly independent before answering candidly. Others hesitate before a specific answer, a pause that rarely shows up when there's nothing being weighed. Once a buyer is confident the researcher has no connection to the vendor, the account often changes: a detail credited as "price" in the vendor's records turns out to be a stalled sales engineer or a demo that never addressed the buyer's actual workflow.

This is the mechanism behind [buyer truth](/glossary/buyer-truth/): the category of finding that only surfaces once the vendor's presence, even indirect, is removed from the room.

## Why This Matters for the Findings

The gap between what a buyer tells a vendor's rep and what they'd tell someone with nothing to sell is where the most actionable intelligence in a win/loss program lives. A finding sourced from a candid third-party interview holds up under scrutiny in a way a vendor-run debrief rarely does, because the account isn't shaped by the buyer's need to manage the relationship in the room.

This isn't limited to lost deals. Customers who signed and are, by most measures, satisfied still hold back specific observations from the vendor's team for the same relationship-preserving reasons that shape a loss debrief. A satisfied customer with a minor but real criticism has no incentive to raise it in a renewal call, since nothing about the relationship requires it and the comment could introduce friction into an otherwise smooth partnership. That same customer, asked independently and off the record, will often volunteer the observation freely, precisely because there's no ongoing relationship in the room to protect.
