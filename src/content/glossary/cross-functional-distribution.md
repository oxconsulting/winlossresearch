# Cross-Functional Distribution

Cross-functional distribution is the practice of routing win/loss findings to every function that has a use for them, rather than treating sales enablement as the default and only destination. Win/loss programs are often positioned as sales enablement from the outset, and scoped, analyzed, and delivered accordingly. That framing captures competitive intel, objection handling, and rep coaching, which have value. It also represents a fraction of what a full set of independent buyer interviews actually contains.

Routing findings exclusively through the sales org is how most of that remaining value gets left behind, usually as the default path of least resistance once a program is scoped as a sales tool from the start rather than a deliberate choice to withhold it.

## What Each Function Needs From the Same Research

Marketing needs to hear how buyers felt about the company and product before the evaluation started, what shifted their perception during it, and what they were left thinking once the decision was made, because that's where messaging either builds momentum or loses it. Product needs to hear about the evaluation step where confidence in the roadmap broke down, or the moment an integration story stopped holding up under scrutiny. Customer success needs to hear what post-sale expectations were set during the evaluation that nobody corrected before the deal closed.

Each function needs to interrogate the same research base, because the signal looks different depending on who's asking the question. A finding that reads as a competitive loss to sales can read as a positioning gap to product marketing and an onboarding risk to CS, all from the same set of interviews.

## Who Owns Making Distribution Happen

Distribution doesn't happen automatically once a report exists. It requires someone with the mandate and the visibility across functions to make sure each team actually sees the findings relevant to them, not just a summary slide. That's usually the person who commissioned the research in the first place, most often a CMO with sight lines across the GTM org. Without an owner, the default outcome is that findings reach whichever function requested the research and stop there.

## A Concrete Example

A company runs a win/loss cycle scoped as sales enablement. The findings get turned into battlecard updates and rep coaching notes. Buried in the same interview set, unrouted anywhere else, is a recurring theme: buyers were confused about implementation timeline during the evaluation, a detail with direct relevance to product and CS but zero relevance to a battlecard. Because distribution stopped at sales, that finding never reaches the teams that could act on it, and the same onboarding confusion shows up in the next research cycle as if it were new.
