# Influencer

An influencer is a stakeholder in a buying committee who shapes the purchase decision through internal credibility or subject-matter expertise, rather than through budget authority or day-to-day contact with the vendor. Influencers are frequently the people other committee members ask before forming an opinion, which gives them outsized weight in a decision despite sitting outside the roles a vendor's sales process is built to track.

## Distinct From the Champion and the Economic Buyer

A [deal champion](/glossary/deal-champion/) drives the sales relationship. An [economic buyer](/glossary/economic-buyer/) controls the budget. An influencer does neither, and that is precisely what makes the role easy for a vendor to miss. Influencers often hold a technical, operational, or security-adjacent role: an engineering lead whose opinion on integration risk carries weight with the CTO, a longtime employee whose experience with a prior vendor colors how a new evaluation gets read, or a peer in another department who has already been through a similar purchase.

None of these people typically join a vendor demo. Their influence travels through the committee's internal conversations, the ones a sales team has no visibility into by design. A champion who is genuinely excited about a product can still lose the internal argument if an influencer the champion respects raises a credible concern, because the champion's own confidence is partly borrowed from that influencer's opinion in the first place.

## Why Influencer Identification Is Hard From Outside

Vendors have no reliable way to identify an influencer from CRM data, because the role is defined by internal standing rather than title or contact frequency. Two companies of similar size and structure can have completely different influencers: at one, it might be a finance director whose skepticism about vendor claims is well known internally; at another, it might be a well-regarded individual contributor whose technical judgment the whole team defers to. Org charts and contact lists show reporting lines, not credibility, and credibility is what actually drives influence inside a committee.

This is one of the clearest cases where independent win/loss interviews add value a sales process cannot replicate on its own. A buyer describing a decision after the fact will often name the specific person whose opinion tipped the room, in a way that never showed up as a contact in the vendor's CRM and never surfaced as a concern in any call the vendor was part of. Identifying who that person tends to be, by role or function, across a pattern of deals is a more durable finding than trying to spot them live on any single account.
