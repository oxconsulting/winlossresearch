# Managed Conversation

A managed conversation is one in which a buyer knows the vendor, or someone connected to the vendor, has visibility into what they say. Knowing this, the buyer shapes their answers to protect the relationship rather than to report what actually happened. A sales call, a customer success check-in, and an internally run win/loss debrief are all managed conversations, regardless of how open-ended the questions are or how skilled the person asking them happens to be.

## Why the Label Matters

The term exists to separate two things that look similar on the surface: a conversation where a buyer is asked questions, and a conversation where a buyer answers those questions honestly. Every managed conversation is the first without reliably being the second. The buyer isn't lying. They're doing what most people do when the person listening has a stake in the answer: giving the version that preserves the relationship, avoids friction, and ends the exchange cleanly.

This is distinct from active deception. A buyer in a managed conversation will often answer every question asked, in detail, and still leave out the specific thing that actually drove a decision. A [rep debrief](/glossary/rep-debrief/) is a managed conversation between a sales rep and a buyer they may want to keep the door open with. A customer success check-in is a managed conversation between a customer and the team supporting their account. Even a debrief run by an internal research function, one step removed from the sales team, remains managed if the buyer perceives any connection back to the vendor relationship.

## What Removes the Management

Neutrality removes it. When the person asking has no commission at stake, no ongoing relationship with the buyer, and no connection the buyer can trace back to the vendor, the conversation stops being managed. This is the mechanism behind [third-party win/loss research](/glossary/third-party-win-loss-research/): an [independent win/loss researcher](/glossary/independent-win-loss-researcher/) conducting an interview isn't a more skilled version of a managed conversation. It's a different kind of conversation entirely, because the condition that produces management, a relationship worth protecting, isn't present.

A useful test for whether a conversation is managed: would the buyer say something different if the person asking had absolutely nothing to do with the vendor. If the honest answer is yes, the conversation currently underway is managed, whatever its format.
